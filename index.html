SAFETY FIRST

This is an educational guide, not a substitute for an adult, trained instructor, or local emergency authority. Do not practise fire, foraging, or shelter-building alone. In a real emergency, prioritize rescue and an established safe shelter.

FIELD NOTE / 005
◉
FOOD & FORAGING

What can I eat?

Recognize why wild food is difficult to identify and how to avoid accidental poisoning.

Safer choices

  • Choose sealed, labelled emergency rations or food from a trusted, known source.
  • Eat familiar cultivated produce only when its identity and cleanliness are certain.
  • Keep food protected from insects, animals, dirt, and floodwater.

Do not eat

  • Unknown berries, leaves, roots, seeds, or flowers.
  • Wild mushrooms unless identified in person by a qualified local expert. The safest choice for beginners is not to eat wild mushrooms.
  • Plants or fungi based on a photo, an app, a folk rule, or an online description.
  • Food that is mouldy, spoiled, swollen in its packaging, or contaminated by floodwater.

Important: colour, smell, taste, animal bites, and cooking are not reliable poison tests. Cooking does not make many poisonous mushrooms safe.

FIELD NOTE / 006
≈
DRINKING WATER

Make water safer

Clear-looking water can still contain germs or chemicals. Know the limits of treatment.

Preferred sources

  • Use sealed bottled water or water confirmed safe by local authorities.
  • During an outage, follow the water utility, local health office, or disaster authority's current instructions.
  • Store treated water in clean, covered, food-grade containers.

Treatment basics

  • Use a recognized household treatment method exactly as directed by its public-health instructions or product label.
  • Boiling can kill many disease-causing germs, but it does not remove fuel, pesticides, heavy metals, salt, or other chemical contamination.
  • Portable filters differ: many do not remove viruses or dissolved chemicals. Check the filter's certified capabilities.

Avoid floodwater, seawater, water with a chemical smell or oily sheen, and water near industrial or agricultural runoff. Do not taste water to test it.

FIELD NOTE / 007
⌂
SHELTER & WEATHER

Stay protected

Shelter is about staying dry, avoiding exposure, and choosing a location that will remain safe.

Safer shelter decisions

  • Prefer a sturdy building, designated evacuation centre, or established campsite approved by the responsible authority.
  • A simple lean-to is a common shelter shape in outdoor education: a sloped cover supported by a frame. Treat it as a supervised demonstration, not an emergency construction project for an untrained person.
  • Natural materials such as fallen branches and leaves are sometimes discussed in shelter education, but never cut vegetation, climb trees, or stand beneath dead or hanging branches.
  • Keep clear of riverbanks, drainage channels, cliffs, unstable slopes, flood-prone ground, and trees with damaged limbs.
  • Use a ground barrier and dry insulating layers when available; avoid direct contact with cold, wet ground.

Do not build a shelter where it could collapse, block an evacuation route, damage protected habitat, or expose you to falling branches. Ask a trained adult before any practical activity.

FIELD NOTE / 008
♨
WARMTH & FIRE AWARENESS

Understand fire safety

Fire can provide warmth and help prepare food, but it can also cause burns, smoke inhalation, and wildfires.

Know the risks

  • Only use an existing, designated fire facility where fires are permitted and a responsible adult is supervising.
  • Never start a fire during dry, windy conditions, near vegetation, inside a tent, or where local rules prohibit it.
  • Keep children, pets, loose clothing, and combustible materials away from flames and hot surfaces.
  • Never leave a fire unattended. If it spreads or feels unsafe, move away and contact emergency services.

Other ways to stay warm

  • Move to a dry, sheltered place and replace wet clothing with dry layers when possible.
  • Insulate yourself from cold ground and protect your head and hands.
  • Stay with your group and seek help if someone becomes confused, unusually drowsy, or has uncontrollable shivering.

This guide intentionally does not teach ignition methods or fire-building steps. Learn practical fire skills only through a qualified instructor and with adult supervision.

FIELD NOTE / 009
⌖
NAVIGATION

Know where you are

Good navigation begins before leaving home. Getting lost is easier to prevent than to solve.

Before a trip

  • Tell a trusted person where you are going, who is with you, and when you expect to return.
  • Check weather forecasts, trail conditions, local advisories, and route closures.
  • Carry a map, charged phone, power bank, and a whistle where appropriate.

If you become lost

  • Pause, stay calm, and avoid wandering farther in an attempt to guess the route.
  • Contact your trusted person or emergency services if possible and share your location.
  • Stay in a safe, visible place unless remaining there creates an immediate danger.
FIELD NOTE / 010
✳
WILDLIFE & ENVIRONMENT

Respect the outdoors

Wild animals, insects, weather, and terrain all deserve distance and respect.

Stay safer

  • Observe animals from a distance. Never feed, corner, touch, or attempt to handle wildlife.
  • Keep food sealed and dispose of waste properly so animals are not attracted to campsites.
  • Wear suitable footwear and check yourself for ticks or insect bites after outdoor activities.
  • Avoid unknown plants, nests, burrows, and animals that appear sick or unusually aggressive.
  • Leave protected plants, habitats, and natural features undisturbed.

If bitten or stung, move away from the animal and seek medical advice. Do not try to capture it.

WHEN THE
UNEXPECTED
HAPPENS.

Different emergencies need different responses. Learn local hazards, follow official alerts, and prioritize life and safety over belongings.

↗Typhoons & severe weatherMonitor official forecasts and follow evacuation advice.＋

Before: Check PAGASA bulletins and local government announcements. Secure loose outdoor items, prepare drinking water, medicines, a flashlight, and a charged phone.

During: Stay indoors and away from windows. Move to a safer location if local authorities advise evacuation. Avoid shorelines, rivers, and areas prone to landslides.

After: Wait for official advice before going outside. Watch for fallen power lines, damaged structures, and unstable trees.

Keep following updates from PAGASA and your local disaster-risk-reduction office.

≈FloodingMove away from floodwater and never walk or drive through it.＋

Before: Know whether your home or route is flood-prone. Keep important items, medicines, and emergency supplies ready, and identify a safe place to go.

During: Move to higher ground when instructed. Never enter moving or standing floodwater on foot or in a vehicle; it may hide hazards or be contaminated. Stay away from electrical equipment in wet areas.

After: Return only when authorities say it is safe. Avoid flood-damaged buildings and do not use food or drinking water that may have been contaminated.

If water is rising or you are in immediate danger, contact local emergency services.

⌁EarthquakesPractice “Drop, Cover, and Hold On” where appropriate.＋

Before: Identify safer spots away from windows and heavy objects. Secure shelves and tall furniture, and practice a household communication plan.

During: Drop, Cover, and Hold On. Protect your head and neck and stay under sturdy furniture if available. If you are outside, move to an open area away from buildings, trees, and power lines.

After: Expect possible aftershocks. Check for hazards without entering damaged buildings, and follow instructions from authorities. If near the coast after strong or long shaking, move to higher ground and follow tsunami warnings.

Follow PHIVOLCS and local-authority updates for official earthquake and tsunami information.

＋Power outagesKeep a flashlight, charged power bank, and safe lighting ready.＋

Prepare: Keep a flashlight, spare batteries, charged power bank, drinking water, and essential medicines where you can find them.

During: Use battery-powered lights when possible. Keep refrigerators and freezers closed. Unplug sensitive electronics if power surges are a concern, and keep clear of fallen wires.

Generator safety: Never operate a generator, charcoal stove, or fuel-burning equipment indoors or in an enclosed space. Follow the manufacturer's safety instructions and local emergency guidance.

When power returns: Check appliances and food safety. Discard food that may have become unsafe rather than tasting it.

Report downed power lines to the electric utility or emergency services; do not approach them.

Always follow current instructions from local emergency services and official disaster-management authorities.
